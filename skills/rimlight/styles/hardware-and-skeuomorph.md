# Hardware and skeuomorph

A real object in its real materials, and usually the whole icon is that
object's face: a camera's front plate, a TV bezel, a cassette window, a
notebook's corner. It suits apps that stand in for a physical thing or
descend from one, such as cameras, players, radios, clocks, instruments and
journals. When it fails, it fails as a catalogue photo. Choose something
else when the app has no physical ancestor worth naming or the object would
only be a prop. The seam with Soft 3D object is finish and intent. Soft 3D
is simplified, matte and blank; this style keeps the leather grain, the
knurling, the drilled holes and the engraved marks.

## Anchors

- Hipstamatic makes the camera body the tile: satin black plates, a crackled
  leatherette belt, one lens, one yellow flash dot.
- (Not Boring) Camera is the restrained version, an off-white matte body and
  a knurled, engraved lens at 64% of the width.
- L8R makes the tile a brushed-aluminium camera front, one big lens in a
  milled steel ring with engraved markings and violet glass in two small
  windows.
- Walkstar is a Walkman face in grainy charcoal plastic, a cassette window
  over one rainbow LED bar and a speaker grille.
- MoniCon is a sage rubber key sunk into cream housing with 21 holes, the
  quiet end of the style.
- Dion is a TV bezel around a bulged CRT of colour bars. All the saturation
  lives on the screen.
- Poolsuite FM crops a radio face, keeping the display whole and running a
  mint button off the edge.
- Yume is a rotated notebook corner with one page curling, standing for the
  whole book.

## Rendering standard

> Rendering: a real object in faithful materials, lit like a product
> designer's hero shot of their own hardware (Braun, Leica, Sony Walkman,
> Teenage Engineering), seen dead-on in front elevation with no perspective.
> The object's face fills the image and its body runs to the edge, finished
> in a soft rolled bevel with a thin highlight along the top that turns to
> shadow along the bottom. [Body material, exact colour, finish], textured at
> the real scale of its grain, new and clean. Soft overhead light from
> slightly left; the body is one hue that darkens 10 to 20 percent toward the
> bottom. Raised parts get a crisp highlight on the upper edge and a short
> soft contact shadow; recessed parts get a shadowed upper wall and a lit
> lower lip. Only glass and polished metal carry sharp speculars, and lens
> glass gets one white point upper left and one violet coating reflection
> opposite. Fine detail (knurling, grille holes, engraved ticks, leather
> grain) stays in one or two zones and the rest of the face is plain. One
> small accent colour. No gloss sheen over the whole, no lens flare, no
> reflected room, no text except words quoted here.

## Composition

- In about two thirds of the corpus the tile is the face, drawn as a front
  elevation with the body edge to edge and the hero feature inside.
- Crop when the whole object is too long or too familiar. Run the body off
  one to three edges and keep the hero whole (Poolsuite's display, Bebo's
  badge, Clasp's lens).
- An object on a ground (Edjing, My Microphone) is the weakest form. If you
  use it, the ground is a material like felt or cork and the object spans
  at least 80% of the width.
- One hero feature. A lens, dial or disc spans 55 to 70 percent of the
  width, a screen or window 70 to 90. Lenses centre at 50 to 65 percent of
  the height under a top band of flash and buttons; screens take the upper
  55 to 65 percent over a knob or grille (CouchTimes, Drumroll).
- One to three secondary parts, each a size step down, counted in the
  prompt. Five kinds of part, body included, is the ceiling.
- Outside the one or two detail zones the face is plain body or one even
  texture, like Hipstamatic's leatherette.
- One dominant silhouette. When the tile is the face, the tile is that
  silhouette and the hero inside it has to read as a plain circle, rounded
  rectangle or band. The body is the background.

## Colour

The body is one material colour over 70 to 90 percent of the tile:
near-black, charcoal, off-white, cream, kraft or sage. One saturated accent
takes under 5 percent, a flash dot or an LED or a needle. Full-spectrum
colour goes only inside a screen, window or lens coating, where it reads as
light. Body gradients hold the hue and darken downward; Package Tracker's
kraft runs #e29562 to #c4713b, Walkstar's charcoal #454448 to #212123. A
brand colour becomes the body material or the accent, never a wash behind
the device.

- Hipstamatic, #242326 #fbc828 #817a78 #c79035. Near-black body, yellow
  flash, warm grey leatherette.
- Package Tracker, #fde3c8 #cf7e4d #b4612a #e59363. One hue family; the label
  is the only neutral.
- (Not Boring) Camera, #ebeae6 #252420 #84778f #f4c474. Off-white body, black
  lens, violet and amber only in the glass.
- MoniCon, #c2ccb8 #b3bda8. Two sage tints and dark holes.

## Never

- A three-quarter product angle, or a view down onto the object.
- A small device floating on an empty gradient with a drop shadow.
- Detail everywhere: screws in every corner, cables, stickers, extra
  switches, controls the real object doesn't have.
- A gloss sheen or glare stripe across the whole tile. Gloss is for glass
  and polished metal.
- Stock-photo light: lens flares, sparkles, chromatic fringes, a rim-lit
  object in a black void.
- Invented text. Generators fill labels and lens rings with gibberish, so
  quote the exact words or ask for unlettered ticks.
- Duplicated parts, such as a second lens or two flash dots. Give counts.
- Wear and grime. L8R's faint scratches are the limit and generators
  overshoot, so say new and clean.
- Texture at the wrong scale, like leather as crumpled foil or grain as JPEG
  noise.
- A UI screenshot on a screen. Show one glowing image, a colour field or a
  pixel glyph.

## Example prompt

    App icon for Longwave, an app for small independent radio stations. The
    whole icon is the front face of a 1960s pocket transistor radio, seen
    straight on. The upper 45 percent holds a recessed tuning window 80
    percent of the width behind clear glass, with a cream dial scale,
    #efe6d2, two rows of unlettered black ticks and one thin red needle,
    #d8452b, right of centre. The lower half is a grid of round speaker
    holes, seven across and four down. One knurled brushed-aluminium
    thumbwheel is cut off by the right edge at mid-height. No logo, no text,
    no screws.

    Rendering: a real object in faithful materials, lit like a product
    designer's hero shot of their own hardware (Braun, Leica, Sony Walkman,
    Teenage Engineering), seen dead-on in front elevation with no
    perspective. The object's face fills the image and its body runs to the
    edge, finished in a soft rolled bevel with a thin highlight along the top
    that turns to shadow along the bottom. Sage-green enamelled steel,
    #9fb39a, satin with a fine orange-peel texture at the real scale of its
    grain, new and clean. Soft overhead light from slightly left; the body is
    one hue that darkens 10 to 20 percent toward the bottom. Raised parts get
    a crisp highlight on the upper edge and a short soft contact shadow;
    recessed parts get a shadowed upper wall and a lit lower lip. Only the
    glass and the aluminium carry sharp speculars. Fine detail stays in two
    zones, the dial ticks and the speaker holes, and the rest of the face is
    plain steel. One small accent colour, the red needle. No gloss sheen over
    the whole, no lens flare, no reflected room, no text.

    Transparent background: a real alpha channel outside the icon tile, no
    backdrop, no checkerboard, no cast shadow. Output a PNG with transparency.
